📖 Introduction to PCR

Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) is an in-vitro method for exponential amplification of a target portion of template DNA. It involves the incorporation of nucleotides by DNA polymerase during thermal cycling.

🧬 Key Principle: PCR selectively amplifies a specific DNA region through repeated cycles of denaturation, annealing, and extension, resulting in millions of copies of the target DNA.

🧪 PCR Components

Required Reagents

Reagent Function
Taq DNA Polymerase Heat-stable enzyme that synthesizes new DNA strands
dNTPs Building blocks (nucleotides) for DNA synthesis
Primers (Forward & Reverse) Short DNA sequences that initiate DNA amplification
MgCl₂ Essential cofactor for DNA polymerase activity
PCR Buffer Provides optimal pH and ionic conditions
Nuclease-free Water Diluent and reaction volume adjuster
Template DNA The DNA sample to be amplified
⚠️ Important: Optimal template DNA amounts:
  • Plasmid/Phage DNA: 0.01 - 1 ng per 25 µL reaction
  • Genomic DNA: 0.1 - 1 µg per 25 µL reaction
Higher template amounts increase the risk of non-specific PCR products.

🔥 PCR Cycling Conditions

The thermal cycler follows a programmed temperature profile to amplify DNA. A typical 3-step PCR protocol is shown below:

Step Temperature Duration Cycles
Initial Denaturation 95°C 2-10 min 1
Denaturation 94°C 10-30 sec 30-35
Annealing 50-60°C 20-30 sec
Extension 72°C 30-60 sec
Final Extension 72°C 5-7 min 1
Hold 10°C ∞ 1

🎯 Bacterial Primers (16S rRNA)

27F Primer: 5'-AGAGTTTGATCCTGGCTCAG-3'

1492R Primer: 5'-GGTTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3'

Target: 16S rRNA gene (~1500 bp)

Purpose: Universal bacterial detection

🎯 Fungal Primers (ITS Region)

ITS1 Primer: 5'-TCCGTAGGTGAACCTGCGG-3'

ITS4 Primer: 5'-TCCTCCGCTTATTGATATGC-3'

Target: Internal Transcribed Spacer (ITS) region

Purpose: Fungal-specific detection and identification

📊 PCR Applications in Molecular Biology

  1. DNA Cloning: Amplifying genes for insertion into vectors
  2. Sequencing: Generating DNA for Sanger or Next-Gen sequencing
  3. Genotyping: Identifying genetic variations and mutations
  4. Detection: Identifying pathogens (bacteria, fungi, viruses)
  5. Forensics: DNA fingerprinting and profiling
  6. Gene Expression: RT-PCR for mRNA quantification
