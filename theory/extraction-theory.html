🧪 DNA / RNA Extraction Theory

Principles, methods, and quality assessment of nucleic acid extraction

← Back to Theory Library

📖 Introduction

DNA and RNA extraction is the process of isolating and purifying nucleic acids from biological samples (cells, tissues, or microorganisms). The goal is to obtain high-quality nucleic acids free from proteins, lipids, carbohydrates, and other contaminants that could interfere with downstream applications.

🧬 Key Principle: DNA/RNA extraction involves breaking open cells, removing proteins and other contaminants, and recovering purified nucleic acid for downstream molecular biology applications.

📋 Basic Steps of Nucleic Acid Extraction

The extraction process follows a series of steps common to both DNA and RNA isolation:

  1. Cell Lysis: Breaking the cell membrane and nuclear membrane to release nucleic acids. This can be done using detergents, enzymes, mechanical disruption, or a combination.
  2. Removal of Proteins and Contaminants: Using enzymes (Proteinase K), organic extraction (phenol-chloroform), or binding columns to remove proteins, lipids, and other contaminants.
  3. Precipitation or Binding: Using alcohol (ethanol or isopropanol) to precipitate DNA/RNA, or binding to a silica membrane in spin columns.
  4. Washing: Removing residual contaminants and salts with wash buffers containing ethanol.
  5. Elution: Releasing pure DNA or RNA using nuclease-free water or elution buffer.

🧪 Reagents Required

Common Reagents for DNA & RNA Extraction

Reagent Function
Lysis Buffer Contains detergents (SDS, CTAB) to break cell membranes
Tris-HCl Maintains optimal pH for enzymatic activity
EDTA Chelates Mg²⁺ to inhibit nucleases
NaCl / Potassium Acetate Provides ionic strength for DNA precipitation
Ethanol (70% and absolute) Washes and precipitates nucleic acids
Isopropanol Precipitates DNA/RNA from solution
Nuclease-free Water Elutes and stores purified nucleic acids

DNA-Specific Reagents

Reagent Function
Proteinase K Digests proteins to remove contamination
RNase A Removes RNA contamination from DNA samples
Phenol:Chloroform:Isoamyl Alcohol Organic extraction for protein removal (optional)

RNA-Specific Reagents

Reagent Function
Guanidinium Thiocyanate Inactivates RNases during extraction
β-Mercaptoethanol Inactivates RNases to protect RNA
DNase I Removes DNA contamination from RNA samples

🧬 DNA Extraction Procedure

Step 1: Sample Preparation

  • Collect cells or tissue sample
  • For tissues: grind or homogenize in lysis buffer

Step 2: Cell Lysis

  • Add lysis buffer and Proteinase K
  • Incubate at 55-65°C to break membranes and digest proteins
💡 Tip: Incubation time and temperature are critical for complete lysis. Prolonged incubation may degrade DNA, while insufficient incubation results in low yield.

Step 3: Removal of Proteins

  • Add phenol:chloroform (if using organic extraction)
  • Centrifuge to separate phases
  • Transfer aqueous (upper) layer containing DNA

Step 4: DNA Precipitation

  • Add cold isopropanol or ethanol
  • Mix gently and incubate
  • Centrifuge to pellet DNA

Step 5: Washing

  • Wash DNA pellet with 70% ethanol
  • Centrifuge and discard supernatant
  • Air dry the pellet

Step 6: Elution

  • Dissolve DNA in nuclease-free water or TE buffer
  • Store at -20°C

🧬 RNA Extraction Procedure

⚠️ Important: RNA is more unstable than DNA. RNases are everywhere (on skin, in dust, on surfaces). Use RNase-free reagents, gloves, and work quickly.

Step 1: Sample Homogenization

  • Homogenize cells/tissues in guanidinium-based lysis buffer
  • Work quickly to prevent RNA degradation

Step 2: Phase Separation

  • Add chloroform
  • Centrifuge to separate phases
  • Collect aqueous phase containing RNA

Step 3: RNA Precipitation

  • Add isopropanol
  • Incubate and centrifuge to pellet RNA

Step 4: Washing

  • Wash RNA pellet with 70% ethanol

Step 5: Elution

  • Dissolve RNA in RNase-free water
  • Store at -80°C

📊 Quality and Quantity Assessment

Measuring Nucleic Acid Concentration

Nucleic acid concentration is measured using a spectrophotometer or NanoDrop by measuring absorbance at 260 nm (A₂₆₀).

Purity Ratios

DNA Purity: A₂₆₀ / A₂₈₀ ≈ 1.8

RNA Purity: A₂₆₀ / A₂₈₀ ≈ 2.0

Protein Contamination: A₂₆₀ / A₂₃₀ > 2.0 indicates clean sample

Ratio Pure DNA Pure RNA Contamination Indication
A₂₆₀ / A₂₈₀ ≈ 1.8 ≈ 2.0 < 1.7 = Protein contamination
A₂₆₀ / A₂₃₀ > 2.0 > 2.0 < 2.0 = Salt or organic contamination

Integrity Check

Nucleic acid integrity is checked by agarose gel electrophoresis:

  • DNA: High molecular weight band without smearing indicates intact DNA
  • RNA: Sharp 28S and 18S rRNA bands (2:1 ratio) indicates intact RNA

🔄 DNA vs RNA: Key Differences in Extraction

Feature DNA RNA
Stability Stable Unstable (easily degraded by RNases)
Special Reagents Proteinase K, RNase A Guanidinium thiocyanate, β-Mercaptoethanol
Storage Temperature -20°C -80°C (or -20°C short-term)
A₂₆₀ / A₂₈₀ Ratio ≈ 1.8 ≈ 2.0
Typical Yield High Variable (depends on sample)

📊 Applications of Purified Nucleic Acids

  1. PCR Amplification: DNA detection and amplification
  2. Sequencing: Sanger, Next-Generation Sequencing
  3. Cloning: Inserting genes into vectors
  4. Gene Expression Analysis: RT-PCR, qPCR, RNA-seq
  5. Genotyping: SNP detection, DNA fingerprinting
  6. Pathogen Detection: Bacterial and fungal identification
  7. Epigenetics: Methylation studies
