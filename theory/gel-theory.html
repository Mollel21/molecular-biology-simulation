🧫 Agarose Gel Electrophoresis

Understanding the principle, factors, and visualization of DNA separation

← Back to Theory Library

📖 Introduction

Agarose Gel Electrophoresis is a technique used to separate DNA, RNA, or protein molecules based on their size and charge. Nucleic acid molecules are negatively charged and migrate toward the anode (positive electrode) when an electric field is applied.

🧬 Key Principle: DNA fragments migrate through the agarose matrix at rates inversely proportional to the log10 of their molecular weight. Smaller fragments migrate faster than larger ones.

🔬 Equipment Required

  1. Electrophoresis Chamber - Contains buffer and gel
  2. Power Supply - Provides electrical current
  3. Gel Casting Trays - Hold the gel while it solidifies
  4. Sample Combs - Form wells in the gel for sample loading
  5. UV Transilluminator - Visualizes stained DNA bands
  6. Pipettes - For loading samples (1-100 µL range)
⚠️ Safety Note: Always wear protective eyewear when observing DNA on a transilluminator to prevent eye damage from UV light.

🧪 Reagents Required

Reagent Function
Agarose Forms the gel matrix for separation
TAE or TBE Buffer Maintains pH and provides ions for conductivity
DNA Ladder (Marker) Reference for estimating fragment sizes
Loading Buffer Contains glycerol (for density) and tracking dyes
Ethidium Bromide / SYBR Safe Stains DNA for visualization under UV light
⚠️ Ethidium Bromide Warning: Ethidium bromide is a known mutagen. Always wear gloves when handling and dispose of properly in hazardous waste containers.

📋 Procedure

  1. Prepare the Gel: Mix agarose powder with electrophoresis buffer (0.5-2% agarose depending on fragment size). Heat in a microwave until completely melted.
  2. Add Stain: Add ethidium bromide (final concentration 0.5 µg/mL) or SYBR Safe to the gel solution before pouring.
  3. Pour the Gel: Cool the solution to ~60°C, pour into a casting tray with a comb, and allow to solidify at room temperature (about 20 minutes).
  4. Remove Comb: Carefully remove the comb without tearing the well bottoms.
  5. Set Up the Apparatus: Place the gel in the electrophoresis chamber and cover with buffer.
  6. Load Samples: Mix DNA samples with loading buffer and pipette into the wells.
  7. Run the Gel: Apply current (typically 100V for 20-40 minutes). DNA migrates toward the positive (red) electrode.
  8. Visualize: Place the gel on a UV transilluminator to view DNA bands.
💡 Tracking Dyes:
  • Bromophenol Blue: Migrates at ~300 bp
  • Xylene Cyanol: Migrates at ~4000 bp

📊 Factors Affecting DNA Migration

1. Agarose Concentration

Different agarose concentrations resolve different DNA fragment sizes:

Agarose Concentration Optimal DNA Size Range
0.5% 1,000 - 10,000 bp
0.8% 800 - 8,000 bp
1.0% 500 - 5,000 bp
1.5% 200 - 2,000 bp
2.0% 100 - 1,000 bp

2. Voltage

As voltage increases, larger fragments migrate proportionally faster than smaller fragments. Best resolution for fragments >2 kb: ≤5 volts per cm (distance between electrodes).

3. Buffer Choice

TAE (Tris-acetate-EDTA): Lower ionic strength, good for large DNA, less heat generation

TBE (Tris-borate-EDTA): Higher ionic strength, better for small DNA, more heat generation

⚠️ Important: Using water instead of buffer results in NO DNA migration. Using concentrated buffer can melt the gel.

4. Ethidium Bromide Effect

Ethidium bromide binds to DNA by intercalating between bases, which alters the DNA's mass, rigidity, and therefore its mobility.
